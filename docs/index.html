What it does

An intake machine, not a chatbot

The jobs a front desk does, running around the clock, with every clinic's data kept separate.

Answers precisely

It works out what is actually being asked (how long a treatment takes or how long it lasts, whether it hurts, whether it's safe, what it costs) and answers from 75+ articles whose facts are checked against published sources (ADA, NHS, CDC, NIH, Mayo Clinic, Cleveland Clinic) and from the clinic's own policies. It follows the conversation ("what about veneers?"), handles several questions in one message and typos, and says so when it doesn't know.

Keeps patients safe

Deterministic safety rules run first: swelling spreading to the eye or neck, trouble breathing or a jaw injury get "call 911"; knocked-out teeth, abscesses and severe pain get first aid, the clinic's number and an urgent staff alert. It never diagnoses and never recommends medicines.

Scores intent, with reasons

Every enquiry is sorted into high, medium or low intent with a plain-language rationale, so the team calls the patients worth calling first instead of guessing from a full inbox.

Follows up, then stops

A structured sequence reaches out over the first week and halts the moment the patient replies, books, or opts out. No clinic ever nags a patient who already said yes or no.

Books and logs everything

It offers real open times inside opening hours as one-tap buttons, collects contact details with a short form, and never double-books. Every message is logged with why the assistant said it, so the owner can see exactly what was said and teach it new answers.

Explainable lead scoring

Why this lead, and why now

The score is never a bare number. It comes with the reasons behind it, so the team can trust it and act on it.

High intent

Named a high-value procedure, asked about timing or availability, and left a reachable contact. Call first.

Medium intent

Clear interest but missing a detail, such as timing or budget readiness. Worth a prompt, well-timed follow-up.

Low intent

Early-stage or information-only. Nurtured gently, never pushed, and never allowed to crowd out the ready patients.

Questions

Before you deploy

Is it a real AI?

Yes, and it is built for this job rather than borrowed. It understands intent, typos and texting shorthand, extracts names, phone numbers, emails, dates and times, works out what aspect of a treatment is being asked about, follows the conversation, and picks the sentence in the clinic's knowledge base that answers the question. It is not a generative chatbot, which is deliberate: it can't make up a price, a policy or medical advice. A quality bench of 300+ real patient questions runs on every change.

How does it avoid saying the wrong thing?

Answers come only from the clinic's published articles and settings. Prices are quoted only when the clinic sets them, unfinished policies stay unpublished, safety rules run before anything else, and it refuses to diagnose or recommend medicines. When it isn't sure, it says so and offers the team, and the question is listed for the clinic to teach an answer. Every message is logged.

Does it work for more than one clinic?

Yes. Each practice's leads, appointments, conversations and knowledge base are isolated from every other, and the API tests check that isolation on every change.

What does it cost to run?

One small server and a PostgreSQL database. The AI runs inside the app, so there are no AI API fees at any volume. Email sending uses Resend's API if you add a key; without one, emails are listed in the dashboard instead.

Can I self-host it?

Yes. LeadFlow AI is open source. The repository ships a Dockerfile and a Render blueprint, so you can run it on your own host in a few minutes.

Is patient data handled responsibly?

Conversations are stored per clinic for the follow-up sequence and the audit log, isolated between tenants. See the Privacy Policy for what is kept and for how long. Treat any clinical or payment workflow as subject to your local healthcare rules.
